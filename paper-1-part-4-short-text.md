# UASA English KSSM — Paper 1, Part 4: Short Answer

## 1. Task identity

**Assessment:** Ujian Akhir Sesi Akademik (UASA)  
**Subject:** English KSSM  
**Levels:** Form 1 and Form 2  
**Paper:** Paper 1 — Reading and Use of English  
**Part:** Part 4  
**Primary construct:** Reading comprehension through retrieval of information from one extended text.

Part 4 should **not** be confused with Part 1 “Short Text MCQ”. Part 4 normally presents **one longer text** followed by short-answer comprehension questions.

Published UASA materials consistently label this section as **Short Answer**.

---

# 2. Form 1 specification

## Core format

Form 1 commonly uses:

- **Questions 25–32**
- **8 questions**
- **8 marks**
- approximately **20 minutes**
- one continuous or semi-continuous text
- two question types:
  - Questions **25–28:** True / False
  - Questions **29–32:** short answers extracted from the text
- short answers normally require:

> **no more than five words and/or a number**

This structure appears consistently in Form 1 UASA/model materials.

### Standard instruction pattern

```text
Read the text and answer the questions that follow.

For questions 25 to 28, write True or False for every statement given.

For questions 29 to 32, choose no more than five words
and/or a number from the text for each answer.
```

---

# 3. Form 2 specification

Form 2 Part 4 is more demanding.

Typical format:

- starts at **Question 25**
- commonly extends to **Question 34**
- up to **10 questions**
- one extended text
- answers are normally copied or minimally extracted from the text
- instruction commonly states:

> **Choose no more than five words and/or a number from the text for each answer.**

Unlike Form 1, Form 2 normally **does not depend on a True/False opening section**. Learners answer short-answer items throughout.

Some recent school/UASA-style papers contain only Questions 25–32 and award 8 marks, so the exact number of questions can vary between implementations.

For generation purposes, use the more established **25–34 / 10-item Form 2 pattern** unless the requested paper specification says otherwise.

---

# 4. Key Form 1 vs Form 2 distinction

| Feature | Form 1 | Form 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Main task | Reading comprehension | Reading comprehension |
| Typical questions | 25–32 | 25–34 |
| Typical number | 8 | 10 |
| Opening items | 4 True/False | Usually short answers |
| Remaining items | 4 short answers | Short answers |
| Maximum response | ≤5 words and/or number | ≤5 words and/or number |
| Text complexity | Lower | Higher |
| Retrieval | Mostly explicit | Explicit + light paraphrase/inference |
| Lexical demand | A1–A2 / emerging A2 | A2–B1-ish |
| Typical text structure | Notice, article, factual text, recipe, promotional text | Article, narrative, report, informational text, letter |

A Form 1 model, for example, uses a vegetarian-lasagne text followed by four True/False statements and four extraction questions.

Form 2 examples use longer prose texts on subjects such as saving money, coral reefs, fast fashion and personal experiences.

# 5. Source-text construction

## Form 1

Generate approximately **180–280 words**.

Suitable forms include:

- short informational article
- leaflet
- advertisement
- school programme
- event notice
- recipe/instructions
- travel information
- simple personal account
- factual description

Texts may contain headings, bullet points or simple visual organisation.

A Form 1 task can therefore use both continuous and semi-continuous text. Existing materials include camps, recipes, walkathons, dinosaurs and environmental/tourism information.

## Form 2

Generate approximately **250–400 words**.

Prefer substantially continuous prose:

- article
- personal account
- narrative
- factual report
- explanatory text
- biography
- travel experience
- social/environmental topic
- health/lifestyle article
- letter

The text should normally contain **several paragraphs** and enough information density to support 10 independent questions.

# 8. Form 1 item-generation algorithm

## Questions 25–28: True / False

Generate four declarative statements.

Recommended balance:

```text
2 TRUE
2 FALSE
```

or

```text
1 TRUE
3 FALSE
```

provided the distribution is not predictable across papers.

Each statement should test comprehension rather than trivial word matching.

### TRUE item

Text:

> Participants who register before 10 September will receive a goodie bag.

Item:

> Early registrations will receive a goodie bag.

Answer:

```text
True
```

This kind of paraphrased equivalence appears in UASA-style Form 1 material.

### FALSE item

Text:

> The walkathon will be held at Taman Rimba.

Item:

> The walkathon will take place at Asiana Hospital.

Answer:

```text
False
```

The false statement should differ by **one meaningful proposition**, not by an absurd contradiction.

### Suitable transformations

Use:

- location substitution
- date/time substitution
- quantity substitution
- actor substitution
- reason/result reversal
- inclusion/exclusion
- sequence modification
- modal change

Example:

```text
Text: Provolone cheese is optional.
TRUE: You do not have to use provolone cheese.

FALSE: Provolone cheese must be included.
```

# 9. Form 1 Questions 29–32: Short answer

Generate four sentence-completion or WH-style questions requiring text extraction.

Answers must normally be:

```text
1–5 words
and/or one number
```

Examples:

```text
29 The programme was organised to ____________________.

30 Participants should collect ____________________ during registration.

31 The event will be held at ____________________.

32 Early participants will receive ____________________.
```

Answers should correspond to exact or near-exact strings from the text.

# 10. Form 2 item-generation algorithm

Generate approximately **10 short-answer items**.

A strong distribution is:

```yaml
direct_detail: 4
paraphrased_detail: 2
cause_reason: 1
sequence_or_result: 1
vocabulary_in_context: 2
```

Not every official-style paper includes explicit vocabulary items, but they appear in published UASA-style materials; for example, candidates may be asked to identify the word in the text corresponding to a supplied meaning.

# 11. Form 2 question types

## A. Direct factual retrieval

Text:

> Amir started saving money when he was fourteen.

Item:

```text
At what age did Amir begin saving money?
```

Answer:

```text
14
```

---

## B. Paraphrased retrieval

Text:

> The nearest bank seemed convenient.

Item:

```text
Why did Amir initially prefer Junbank?
```

Answer:

```text
nearest to their house
```

The question paraphrases the proposition but the answer remains recoverable from the passage.

---

## C. Cause → effect

Text:

```text
The beach was closed because strong winds had created dangerous waves.
```

Question:

```text
Why was the beach closed?
```

Answer:

```text
strong winds
```

---

## D. Effect → cause

Question:

```text
What caused dangerous waves?
```

Answer:

```text
strong winds
```

Do not use both questions in the same set unless they test genuinely different information.

---

## E. Sequence

Text:

```text
After calling his parents, Daniel walked to the police station.
```

Question:

```text
What did Daniel do after calling his parents?
```

Answer:

```text
walked to the police station
```

---

## F. Character motivation

Text:

```text
She selected Koibank because it offered the highest interest rate.
```

Question:

```text
Why did she choose Koibank?
```

Answer:

```text
highest rate of interest
```

---

## G. Vocabulary in context

Text:

```text
The entire village joined the celebration.
```

Item:

```text
Which word means "whole"?
```

Answer:

```text
entire
```

Published Form 2 UASA-style material explicitly uses this type.

Use no more than **1–2 vocabulary questions** in a 10-item set.

# 12. Retrieval-distance design

Good Part 4 questions should not all be word-for-word copies.

Use three retrieval levels.

## Level 1 — direct lexical match

```text
Text: She travelled to Penang by bus.

Question: How did she travel to Penang?

Answer: by bus
```

## Level 2 — simple paraphrase

```text
Text: The journey lasted three hours.

Question: How long did the trip take?

Answer: three hours
```

## Level 3 — local inference

```text
Text:
The sky became dark and thunder could be heard.
The organisers quickly moved everyone inside.

Question:
Why were the participants moved indoors?
```

A valid answer must nevertheless have a short textual anchor.

Do not require multi-step reasoning beyond the student's expected reading level.

---

# 13. Answer-length constraint

This is fundamental.

Every generated answer must pass:

```python
word_count(answer_without_number) <= 5
```

Examples:

Valid:

```text
three hours
the school hall
because of heavy rain
at the age of fourteen
RM500
```

Invalid:

```text
because there was very heavy rain outside
```

If the natural answer exceeds five words, rewrite either the text or the question.

---

# 14. Sentence-completion grammar

If using sentence completion, the extracted answer must fit grammatically.

Bad:

```text
The event was organised because __________.
Answer from text: to encourage healthy living
```

The completed sentence becomes:

> because to encourage healthy living

Ungrammatical.

Better:

```text
The event was organised to __________.
```

Answer:

```text
encourage healthy living
```

Always simulate the completed sentence before approving the item.

---

# 15. Question order

Questions should generally follow the **chronological/textual order** of information in the passage.

Recommended:

```text
Q25 → paragraph 1
Q26 → paragraph 1
Q27 → paragraph 2
Q28 → paragraph 2
Q29 → paragraph 3
...
```

Avoid:

```text
Q25 → paragraph 4
Q26 → paragraph 1
Q27 → paragraph 5
Q28 → paragraph 2
```

unless deliberately assessing global reading.

This reduces irrelevant scanning load and resembles standard reading-test construction.

---

# 16. Distractor control for True/False

For Form 1 False items, generate the false proposition from the source using a controlled mutation.

```yaml
mutation_types:
  - NUMBER_CHANGE
  - DATE_CHANGE
  - LOCATION_CHANGE
  - PERSON_CHANGE
  - PURPOSE_CHANGE
  - NEGATION
  - SEQUENCE_CHANGE
  - QUANTITY_CHANGE
```

Example:

```text
Source:
Registration ends on 15 September.

Mutation:
Registration ends on 10 September.
```

Do not create false statements by introducing completely unrelated facts.

---

# 17. Avoid answer overlap

Each question should ideally correspond to a different information unit.

Bad:

```text
Q25 Where did Sarah go?
Q26 Which city did Sarah visit?
```

Both answer:

```text
Melaka
```

Better:

```text
Q25 Where did Sarah go?
Q26 Who travelled with her?
```

---

# 18. Avoid accidental alternative answers

Before accepting an item, search the entire passage for other phrases that could satisfy the question.

Example:

```text
Question:
What did the students receive?
```

Text contains:

- certificates
- free drinks
- souvenirs

The question is ambiguous.

Rewrite:

```text
What did the winners receive after the competition?
```

# 19. Lexical calibration

## Form 1

Prefer:

- common concrete nouns
- high-frequency verbs
- simple adjectives
- familiar phrasal verbs
- short clauses
- explicit cohesive devices

Grammar may include:

- present simple
- past simple
- present continuous
- basic future forms
- modals
- comparatives
- simple relative clauses
- basic conjunctions

Avoid unnecessarily dense nominalisation.

## Form 2

Permit:

- wider adjective range
- common abstract nouns
- phrasal verbs
- relative clauses
- present perfect where natural
- passive constructions
- cause/effect linkers
- more varied subordinate clauses
- moderately unfamiliar vocabulary inferable from context

Do not turn vocabulary difficulty itself into the main construct.

---

# 20. Passage writing rules

The source passage must:

1. be internally coherent;
2. contain enough distinct propositions;
3. avoid factual contradictions;
4. avoid duplicated information unless rhetorically natural;
5. contain all required answers explicitly or locally inferably;
6. use natural English rather than sentences engineered around questions;
7. avoid excessive names and numbers;
8. not require external knowledge;
9. contain plausible real-world content;
10. support the intended number of independent items.

---

# 21. Recommended information density

For Form 1:

```text
8–12 assessable propositions
```

For Form 2:

```text
12–18 assessable propositions
```

Not every proposition needs a question.

This permits the passage to contain **non-assessed information**, preventing it from feeling like a list of answers disguised as prose.

---

# 22. Item difficulty control

Difficulty should primarily come from **reading**, not obscure vocabulary.

Use these levers:

```yaml
easy:
  - exact lexical overlap
  - answer and question close together
  - concrete fact
  - explicit subject

medium:
  - synonym/paraphrase
  - pronoun reference
  - cause-effect relation
  - answer embedded in longer sentence

hard:
  - local inference
  - relationship across adjacent sentences
  - contextual vocabulary
```

Suggested distribution:

### Form 1

```yaml
easy: 4
medium: 3
hard: 1
```

### Form 2

```yaml
easy: 3
medium: 5
hard: 2
```

Do not exceed the expected CEFR level simply to create difficulty.

## Step 5 — generate questions

Apply level-specific rules.

## Step 6 — solve the paper independently

The model must answer each question **using only the completed passage**.

## Step 7 — run ambiguity check

Verify:

```text

```

```text

```

## Step 9 — check textual order

Ensure progression roughly follows the text.

## Step 10 — output question paper + answer key.

# 24. Automated validation rules

An LLM/item generator should reject an item when any of these conditions is true:

```yaml
reject_if:
  - answer_not_in_text
  - more_than_one_plausible_answer
  - answer_exceeds_word_limit
  - completion_is_ungrammatical
  - requires_external_knowledge
  - same_fact_already_tested
  - question_contains_answer
  - false_statement_is_partly_true
  - inference_requires_multiple_unsupported_steps
  - vocabulary_above_level_is_essential_to_answer
```

# 28. Critical distinction from other UASA parts

Do not generate Part 4 like:

### Part 1
Multiple independent short texts + three-option MCQs.

### Part 3
Information transfer into table/mind map/graphic organiser.

### Part 5
Gapped-text matching.

Part 4 is fundamentally:

```text
ONE EXTENDED SOURCE TEXT
        ↓
READ FOR MEANING
        ↓
LOCATE / INTERPRET INFORMATION
        ↓
VERY SHORT TEXTUAL RESPONSE
```

Form 1 additionally uses True/False items before short-answer extraction.

---

# 29. Final generation rule

The goal is **not** merely to write questions whose answers happen to appear in the passage.

A valid UASA-style Part 4 set should require the candidate to:

```text
read
→ locate
→ connect question wording to passage wording
→ identify the relevant proposition
→ extract only the necessary words
```

Therefore, avoid excessive verbatim question-to-text matching.

The ideal item contains a **small lexical or grammatical transformation** between the passage and question while preserving a single unambiguous answer.
