# Form 1 UASA English: Part 3 Information Transfer

Use this guide when the user requests a Form 1 English **Part 3** or **Information Transfer** assessment. It is a reading task, not a writing task: pupils transform a **linear text** into structured information.

## Format to preserve

| Requirement | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Paper | Reading and Use of English |
| Part | Part 3: Information Transfer |
| Input | One CEFR A2 linear text |
| Output | Eight fields in a table or graphic organiser |
| Items and marks | 8 items × 1 mark = 8 marks |
| Scoring | Dichotomous: correct = 1, otherwise = 0 |

The KPM 2025 format explicitly specifies the linear text, OPB format, eight items/eight marks, CEFR A2, and dichotomous scoring. The usual table/graphic organiser, numbering 17–24, about 20 minutes, and the instruction to use no more than three words and/or a number are strong UASA conventions, not claims about the wording of the KPM format document.

## Build the assessment

1. Plan eight target facts before drafting the passage. Use varied, concrete categories such as person, place, time, date, activity, object, price, quantity, reason, feeling, transport, or description.
2. For each fact, define a field label, canonical answer, source span, accepted variants, word count, and any competing information.
3. Write one coherent, connected prose text. Use a personal account, short article, report, description, narrative, or informational passage—not an advertisement, timetable, poster, infographic, or other mainly non-linear text.
4. Present the eight fields as concise semantic prompts, not full comprehension questions. For example, use `Venue: ______` rather than `Where was the event held?`
5. Include the learner instruction: `Read the text below and complete the table. Use no more than three words and/or a number from the text for each answer.`

## Answer and difficulty rules

- Design every answer to be a short source-lifted span, preferably contiguous. Do not require pupils to paraphrase or write full sentences.
- Keep answers to no more than three words and/or a number. Design to this limit; do not write a passage first and hope usable answers emerge.
- Give each field one defensible answer. Make labels specific enough to rule out other plausible details in the passage.
- Keep vocabulary and syntax at CEFR A2. Raise difficulty through comprehension, not obscure vocabulary.
- Include controlled competing information in several items. Useful methods include an original plan versus a final decision, two people or events, two values, a pronoun reference, a chronological change, or a negative/positive contrast.
- Do not rely on layout, typography, or one-off keyword matching. The pupil should need to connect the field label to the right proposition.

Example:

> The meeting was first planned for the school library, but it was moved to Merdeka Hall because the library was being repaired.

| Field | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Venue | Merdeka Hall |

The competing location makes the pupil identify the final venue rather than copy the first place named.

## Key and marking

Create the key while writing the passage. For each item, record:

```text
item: 17
field: Venue
canonical answer: Merdeka Hall
accepted answers: [Merdeka Hall]
source span: Merdeka Hall
mark: 1
```

Apply one mark only for an acceptable answer. Do not award partial credit. Normally require the copied word form, including needed plural forms and proper-noun capitals; avoid making trivial punctuation the main difficulty.

## Final check

Before delivery, confirm:

- There are exactly eight gaps, worth eight marks in total.
- The passage is linear and approximately CEFR A2.
- Each canonical answer appears in the source, is no more than three words and/or a number, and is preferably a contiguous span.
- Every field has one defensible answer; no two items assess the same fact.
- The fields cover varied semantic categories and need no outside knowledge.
- Several items contain competing information and cannot be completed reliably by superficial keyword matching.
- The learner version, answer key, item numbering, accepted variants, and scoring all agree.

## Evidence and wording boundary

Use the KPM format document for the official format. Treat the three-word limit, table/graphic-organiser response, source-lifted answers, suggested numbering/time, and related item-writing practices as UASA-aligned conventions supported by model and teacher materials. In `notes.md`, distinguish these two evidence levels.
