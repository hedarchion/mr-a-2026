# Instructional Notes - Form 2 Paper 1 Part 5 Worksheets

## Purpose and conditions

Use these two worksheets after the *Find the Missing Link* presentation. They are brief individual formative reading checks, not full summative UASA papers. Give pupils about 12-15 minutes per worksheet. Ordinary classroom supports are allowed, but pupils should work independently and choose one letter for each gap. Award one mark per correct answer: six marks in total.

The assessed reading habits are the four taught in the deck:

1. Identify the topic.
2. Follow who or what the paragraph is about.
3. Read the sentence before and after the gap.
4. Predict the missing idea before committing to an option.

## Suggested delivery routine

1. Before handing out the sheet, display or say: “Do not choose an option because it has the same word. Choose the option that connects the ideas.”
2. Read only the directions aloud. Do not read the passage or options aloud for pupils.
3. Tell pupils to lightly mark any two options that they think are extra only after they have tried all six gaps.
4. At 8 minutes, prompt: “Check both sides of every gap. Does your choice make the next sentence natural?”
5. Collect the work, or mark it together after individual completion.

## Worksheet 1 - The Festival Film

**Key:** 35 G, 36 D, 37 A, 38 H, 39 C, 40 B. Unused: E, F.

| Gap | What to listen for | Correct reasoning | Likely misconception and repair |
|---|---|---|---|
|35|Planning before filming|G gives the preparation that must happen before the group meets to film.|A pupil may choose E because it mentions the camera. Ask: “Does it explain why they are ready to film?”|
|36|Problem to solution|D solves the low-battery problem and leads to the waiting time.|Ask: “What must happen before they can begin interviews?”|
|37|Action and continuity|A explains how they film while customers continue shopping.|Ask: “Who continues working in the next sentence?”|
|38|Cause and consequence|H responds to the bus noise; the next sentence confirms a clear second recording.|Ask: “Why do they record again?”|
|39|Suggestion and reaction|C is an ending idea, so “Everyone agreed” has a clear referent.|Ask: “What did everyone agree to?”|
|40|Sequence|B follows choosing and ordering clips, then precedes showing the finished film.|Ask: “What happens after filming but before a film is shown?”|

## Worksheet 2 - A Small School Garden

**Key:** 35 F, 36 A, 37 H, 38 C, 39 G, 40 D. Unused: B, E.

| Gap | What to listen for | Correct reasoning | Likely misconception and repair |
|---|---|---|---|
|35|Reference: “This”|F names the plan; “This helped them decide where each plant should go.”|Ask: “What does ‘This’ mean?”|
|36|Cause and effect|A provides the soil problem that compost and stone removal solve.|Ask: “Why would they mix compost and remove stones?”|
|37|Problem and solution|H explains why the club moves mint into pots.|Ask: “Why does mint need more space?”|
|38|Absence and solution|C establishes the holiday absence; the security guard solves it.|Ask: “Who will not be able to water the plants?”|
|39|Positive result and response|G naturally follows a healthy garden and leads to harvesting herbs.|Ask: “How would the pupils feel when they see new growth?”|
|40|Main point|D states the project lesson and supports the future care sign.|Ask: “Is this an event, or the lesson from all the events?”|

## Feedback and next teaching decision

- **5-6 marks:** Ask pupils to explain one answer using two pieces of evidence. Give the other worksheet as independent transfer.
- **3-4 marks:** Re-teach the before-and-after bridge with Gaps 36 and 38 from either worksheet. Contrast the correct option with one topical distractor.
- **0-2 marks:** Return to one three-sentence context from the deck. Model topic filtering first, then have pupils underline the person/thing or connector that proves the final choice.

## Limitations

The worksheets give a small sample of Part 5 reading cohesion. They do not establish reliable summative attainment and should not be used alone for high-stakes decisions.
