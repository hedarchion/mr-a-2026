## Objective

Students will build 3–4 simple family sentences by combining **WHO + ACTION + MORE**, using familiar pronouns (`I/me/my`, `you/your`, `he/him/his`, `she/her`, `we/us/our`, `they/them/their`) and adding a relevant object, person, place, time, or together-phrase.

## Prerequisite and likely misconceptions

- Prerequisite: family nouns, simple present / familiar action vocabulary, and the prior subject–object–possessive pronoun jobs.
- Likely misconceptions: starting with an object pronoun (`Him I play...`), placing extra information before WHO, forgetting `-s` in a simple present model with `he/she`, using `my/our/his` without a following noun, or treating every sentence as a pronoun blank.
- The deck responds by repeatedly reconstructing whole sentences, delaying answer reveals, modelling `help → helps` without a grammar lecture, and returning to `WHO → ACTION → MORE`.

## Slide map and teacher moves

1. **How long can we make it?** Build `She → She plays → ...` one chunk at a time. Ask whether “She” is a sentence; accept the class’s ideas and establish that a sentence can grow.
2. **Meet our building words.** Point randomly to chunks. Students make any sensible three-part sentence. Click choices if useful; oral pointing is the non-digital fallback.
3. **Sentence machine.** Students choose one WHO, ACTION and WHAT/WHO. Check that the action comes after WHO. The displayed feedback models `He helps her.`.
4. **Make it bigger.** Students tap or suggest optional chunks. Ask which questions the extra chunks answer: what, where, when, with whom.
5. **I / me / my.** Reveal one sentence at a time. Keep the explanation functional: `I` starts, `me` follows the action, `my` comes before a family thing.
6. **We / us / our.** Repeat the same construction pattern for more than one person.
7. **Who should start?** Students choose the subject starter only, then add an action orally. Expected sequence: `I, she, we, they`.
8. **Build it twice.** Build first with the family noun, then replace the repeated noun with a pronoun. Accept the exact models or other sensible versions.
9. **Add one more idea.** Students expand `She reads` with WHAT, WHERE, WHEN and WITH WHO. The goal is generative sentence growth, not one fixed answer.
10. **Sentence puzzle.** Students reorder orally before revealing. Ask what normally comes first; expected answer: WHO, then ACTION.
11. **Fix the broken sentence.** Students rebuild the complete sentence, not just identify one wrong word.
12. **Picture to sentence.** Ask students to choose one person and say WHO + ACTION + MORE. Accept familiar structures students already know, including present continuous if they use it naturally; do not stop production for a new tense explanation.
13. **Build three sentences.** Give 30 seconds thinking time, then partner rehearsal. Collect 2–3 examples and invite a longer version.
14. **No word bank.** Students create one sentence for each prompt. Reveal only after attempts. End by asking, “Can you make it longer?”

## Grammar grounding

Canonical private reference: `grammar for english teachers.md.md`.

- `Types of sentence constituent > Additional factors and related issues > Different kinds of pronoun`, lines **12740–12751**: subject pronouns include `I, he, she, we, they`; object/complement forms include `me, him, her, us, them`; `you` can be subject or object.
- `24 Ellipsis and substitution > Substitution > Replacing nouns and noun phrases with pronouns and possessive adjectives`, lines **15393–15426**: pronouns and possessive adjectives avoid repeating clear information; lists subject pronouns, object pronouns and possessive adjectives.
- `Types of sentence constituent > Form > Noun phrases`, lines **12274–12316**: a pronoun or noun can function as a noun phrase, and possessive adjectives occur before the noun they determine.
