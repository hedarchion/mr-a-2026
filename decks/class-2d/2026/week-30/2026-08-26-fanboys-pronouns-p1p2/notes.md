## Objective

Students replace clear family-member nouns with the correct subject, object and possessive-adjective forms and interpret short Family Day texts with at least 12 of 16 worksheet marks.

## Lesson sequence

1. Retrieve the recorded FANBOYS bridge (`and`) and identify family vocabulary.
2. Model `he`, `she`, and `they` as the person who does the action.
3. Contrast `he/him`, `she/her`, and `they/them` using action direction.
4. Add `his/her/their + noun` for ownership.
5. Build and retrieve the full three-job pattern.
6. Exit check: `He is my brother. I play with him. His ball is red.`

Likely misconceptions: object pronoun used as the subject; subject pronoun used after a verb or preposition; `his/her/their` used without a following noun; confusing `her` as object versus possessive adjective; missing the plural signal for `they/them/their`.

## Grammar grounding

Canonical private reference: `grammar for english teachers.md.md`.

- `Types of sentence constituent > Additional factors and related issues > Different kinds of pronoun`, lines 12740–12751: distinguishes pronouns normally used as subjects (`I`, `he`, `she`, `we`, `they`) from object/complement forms (`me`, `him`, `her`, `us`, `them`).
- `24 Ellipsis and substitution > Substitution > Replacing nouns and noun phrases with pronouns and possessive adjectives`, lines 15393–15426: explains that pronouns and possessive adjectives avoid repeating information that is already clear and lists the subject, object, possessive-adjective and possessive-pronoun systems.
- `Types of sentence constituent > Adverbials > Coordinating conjunctions`, lines 12651–12672: records `and`, `but`, and `or` as core coordinating conjunctions; only `and` is retrieved as the bridge from the latest recorded lesson.

The visible lesson uses the child-friendly jobs `does`, `receives`, and `owns + thing`, while teacher notes retain the formal labels subject pronoun, object pronoun and possessive adjective.
