# Poetry Is Easier Than You Think — 1M — slide notes

## 1. 1m-b1-poem (choice)

HOOK / DIAGNOSTIC (~3 min, whole class). Gesture vote first (fingers 1/2/3), then take 3 hands max: 'What makes you think so?' Accept one-word answers. Log rhyme-rule assumption silently; do not correct yet. Sayable bridge: 'Let's meet a real poet.' Baseline for Beat 18 comparison.

## 2. 1m-b2-swing (choice)

AUTHENTIC MODEL (~4 min). Read excerpt aloud TWICE; pupils chorally repeat the second line before voting. Expect near-unanimous B; D signals over-inference. Follow-up oral (no writing): 'Could you write about something ordinary — football, rain, your cat?' Collect 4–5 ideas. Bridge: 'If a swing can be a poem, what about rhyme?'

## 3. 1m-b3-rhyme (choice)

MISCONCEPTION CORRECTION (~2 min). A/B fingers, fast vote. If A: class says the pair aloud once; no phonics lecture. Log rhyme-as-rule correction. Bridge: 'If rhyme is not the secret, what makes these lines interesting?' Credit on slide is short; full Sandburg attribution follows on next slide.

## 4. 1m-b4-fog (passage)

FULL POEM EXPERIENCE (~4 min). Read aloud once at normal speed, once slowly, pointing at each line. Pupils repeat 'sits / looks / moves' chorally. Accept any reasonable image (cat, smoke, quiet animal sitting); do NOT define personification yet. Word bank (voiceable rescue): fog · cat feet · sits · looks · moves. Teacher-spoken close: 'He does not explain fog. He makes us imagine it.' Bridge: 'Fog cannot really sit. So what did the poet do?'

## 5. 1m-b5-feet (choice)

FIRST TOOL, DISCOVERED (~4 min). After vote, show orally: 'The wind whispers. The moon watches. The rain dances.' Define pupil wording: personification gives a non-human thing a human or living action (animal OK at A2). Try-it oral with verb bank first (sleeps / waits / smiles): 'The door ___. The sun ___.' Counters 'poem must be literally true'. Bridge: 'Poets also choose small exact words.'

## 6. 1m-b6-picture (choice)

SPECIFICITY CONTRAST (~3 min). Key line to say: 'Specific words make pictures.' Counter 'nice is enough' and 'poetic means long words': 'Did B use difficult English? No — exact English.' Try-it oral with frame: 'The dog ___.' (sleeps / waits / barks / watches). Bridge: 'Now watch one plain sentence become a poem.'

## 7. 1m-b7-bell (steps)

MODEL SENTENCE→POEM (~4 min). Build ORALLY before each reveal; offer verb choices (rings / shouts / sings) rather than open elicitation. Sayable per step: 'What can we add? What stronger verb? Where does it shout?' Accept variants (cries / hot evening). No grammar correction mid-build. Close: 'We did not need difficult English.' Bridge: 'Those moves have names. Here are all five together.'

## 8. 1m-b8-toolbox (passage)

SYNTHESIS REFERENCE (~3 min, no new teaching, nothing hidden). All five visible from the start. Point to each; pupils repeat each example chorally (fix whispers, repetition). Point back: 1 = rain, 2–4 just now, 5 = bell break. Sayable: 'Poetry is making choices with ordinary words.' Bridge: 'Now test yourself — which tool is this?'

## 9. 1m-b9a-moon (choice)

CLASSIFY 1/3 (~1.5 min). Whole-class finger vote (1/2/3) before reveal. Contrast: not simile — no like/as.

## 10. 1m-b9b-brother (choice)

CLASSIFY 2/3 (~1.5 min). Expected confusion: 'wind is non-human' → counter: 'Who runs? The brother — a person. The tool here is like.' Not personification.

## 11. 1m-b9c-run (choice)

CLASSIFY 3/3 (~1 min). Contrast: no comparison word, no non-human action. Total Beat 9 ≈ 4 min.

## 12. 1m-b10-camera (prompt)

GUIDED IDEA GENERATION (~5 min, oral only). Sayable: 'Do not invent a new world. Tell me what is really here.' Build bank publicly from pupil words (take 6–8): wet shoes · grey sky · noisy rain · cold floor · umbrellas · dripping bags · sleepy students · water on the window. Accept feelings (sleepy, lonely). Per-prompt rescue: See — shoes, sky, windows. Hear — rain, voices, bells? Feel — cold? sleepy? wet socks? Notice — water on glass, dripping bags. Keep bank visible into Beat 11. Bridge: 'Now use these words. Let's write one together.'

## 13. 1m-b11-rain (steps)

GUIDED CONSTRUCTION (~5 min, teacher holds the pen). Staged sayables: 'What does the rain do? Where? What does it sound like? If rain were a person — waits? listens? watches?' Verb choices for 1M (taps / knocks / sings); simile frame 'It sounds like ___.' Title decided aloud ('What shall we call it? One word.'). Load-bearing line, say verbatim: 'This is not a perfect poem. It is a poem because we made choices.' Counters one-correct-poem; accept variant verbs. Leave visible as confidence anchor; move OFF this slide before independent writing so pupils cannot copy.

## 14. 1m-b12-one (prompt)

COMMIT (~2 min). Visible commitment in 60 seconds (finger on choice / whisper to neighbour). Steer strugglers to RAIN / SCHOOL / MOON (banked language exists). Sayable: 'You only need ONE subject.' Teacher line: 'One clear subject makes a stronger poem.' Bridge: 'Here is a recipe if you need steps.'

## 15. 1m-b13a-recipe (steps)

RECIPE PART 1/2 (~1 min). Read aloud once. Stress 'training wheels, not rules.' Link to toolbox (line 5≈simile, 6≈personification, 7≈repetition). Do NOT collect writing yet. Bridge: 'Plan in 30 seconds, then write.'

## 16. 1m-b13b-recipe (steps)

RECIPE PART 2/2 (~1 min). Same wording as storyboard; split only because steps caps at 4 items and TV legibility needs large type. Total Beat 13 ≈ 2 min (trimmed from 3 to hold the hour).

## 17. 1m-b14-plan (prompt)

MODEL PLANNING (~2 min hard cap). Model with rain in ≤60 sec, then pupils fill SILENTLY. Circulate for blanks: first check = subject + one notice. Do NOT exceed 2 min. Sayable: 'Copy the frames. Use your own words. Thirty seconds.' Teacher close: 'Notes first, lines second. Now turn your notes into lines.' Bridge: 'Now write. Six to eight lines.'

## 18. 1m-b15-write (writing)

INDEPENDENT TRANSFER (~8 min hard stop, timer, SILENCE). Sayable: 'Start now. One subject. Your choices.' Circulate in ladder order: one clear subject → lines appearing → one concrete detail → one tool. For non-starters, point to Beat 14 frames and dictate a starter ('My ___ ...'). Light grammar correction only — never block writing. Rescue ladder (voiceable): 'What can you see? What does it do? What does it sound like? What is it like? If it were a person, what would it do?' EXTENSION only if finished early: 'Repeat your best line once more.' Evidence: titled 6–8 line poem.

## 19. 1m-b16-check (prompt)

SELF-CHECK / REVISE (~2 min: fast check, then rewrite one line). Sayable: 'Do not ask, Is it perfect? Ask, Which line can I make better?' Affirm revisions adding specificity or a tool even if grammar is imperfect. Teacher line: 'Poets revise choices; one stronger line is enough today.' Bridge: 'So what IS poetry, after doing it?'

## 20. 1m-b17-what (choice)

CONSOLIDATION (~1 min). Expect C; if A/B, replay evidence ('comes/feet did not rhyme; cold/taps were not hard'). Follow-up oral, 3 answers: 'What is one choice you made? (a simile / a strong verb / a repeated word / a line break)'. Do NOT re-teach toolbox. Bridge: 'One last challenge — prove it in two lines.'

## 21. 1m-b18-moon (writing)

EXIT EVIDENCE, FRESH TRANSFER (~2 min). Reveal the frame only for stuck pupils (press Reveal). Reward ANY real choice — human action, comparison, strong break — not one perfect answer. Possibles: 'The moon / watches me.' or 'The bright moon hangs / like a lamp in the sky.' Take 3–4 shares; for each ask class: 'What choice did the writer make?' Do NOT judge 'beautiful'. Final line, say verbatim: 'You have written a poem.' Collect exit lines with Beat 15 poems.
