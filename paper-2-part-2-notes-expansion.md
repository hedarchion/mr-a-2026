# Form 1 UASA English: Paper 2 Part 2 Notes Expansion

Use this guide when the user requests a Form 1 English **Notes Expansion** assessment, model answer, lesson, or marking support. The task is guided writing: pupils turn short prompts into a connected composition. It is not information transfer, copying, or sentence transformation.

## Format to preserve

| Requirement | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Paper | 12/2 Writing |
| Part | Part 2: Notes Expansion |
| Task | One subjective open-response question |
| Marks | 20 |
| Target | CEFR A2–B1 |
| Usual Form 1 convention | About 90 words; three notes in a bubble map or similar organiser |

The KPM 2025 format identifies one 20-mark Notes Expansion task and an A2–B1 target. The 90-word target and three-note organiser are strong UASA-aligned Form 1 conventions, not fixed rules claimed in the public format document.

## Build the task

1. Choose a familiar, age-appropriate context: school life, hobbies, family, celebrations, health, sport, technology, holidays, food, environment, or community.
2. Set a clear purpose and text type. Use a short essay/composition by default; use an article, recount, description, opinion piece, or informal letter only when the context supports it.
3. Create three distinct, expandable prompts. Each should require a different idea, such as what/who/where, reason/example, and effect/opinion/action.
4. Check that pupils can answer all prompts naturally in about 90 words without specialist knowledge or advanced grammar.
5. Use a concise UASA-style instruction:

```text
In your class, you have been talking about [topic].

In about 90 words, write an essay about [specific task].
You may use the notes below to help you.

[central topic and three notes]

Write your essay.
```

## Prompt and language rules

- Treat notes as content anchors, not sentences to copy. A response should answer, develop, and connect each idea.
- Make each note distinct and relevant to the title. Avoid prompts that ask the same thing in different words.
- Prefer direct question prompts: `What...?`, `Who...?`, `Where...?`, `When...?`, `Why...?`, `How...?`, `What should...?`, or `How will...?`
- Keep the topic and language accessible at A2–B1. Use familiar vocabulary and let difficulty arise from generating details, reasons, examples, and effects.
- Do not provide enough detail for pupils merely to copy the stimulus. Allow relevant invented supporting details.
- Treat “about 90 words” as a target, not an exact-count requirement. Aim model answers at roughly 85–105 words without padding.

Example prompt set:

```text
Topic: A Healthy Weekend

• What activity can you do?
• How can you encourage your friends?
• How can this improve your life?
```

## Model-answer expectations

A suitable Form 1 model answer normally has a brief introduction, one developed idea for each prompt, simple links such as `and`, `but`, `because`, `so`, `also`, `first`, `next`, and `finally`, and a short conclusion or final opinion. It should be connected prose, not a numbered response. Do not force a five-paragraph essay.

## Marking

Use the UASA/CEFR-aligned four-domain model, 5 marks each:

| Domain | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Content | All notes are addressed with relevant ideas. |
| Communicative achievement | Purpose, text type, tone, and reader needs are reasonably met. |
| Organisation | Ideas are logically ordered and linked. |
| Language | A2–B1 vocabulary and grammar communicate meaning with reasonable control. |

Total: 20 marks. When marking a pupil response, give brief evidence-based feedback for each domain and identify the most useful next step.

## Final check

Before delivery, confirm:

- The task has one clear purpose, suitable text type, and three distinct notes.
- Every note is relevant, answerable, and expandable; no note duplicates another.
- The task needs no outside knowledge and can be completed naturally in about 90 words.
- The topic, prompts, expected language, and model answer suit Form 1 A2–B1 learners.
- The model answer develops rather than copies the notes, covers all prompts, and is coherent.
- The learner task, mark allocation, rubric, model answer, and any answer key agree.
